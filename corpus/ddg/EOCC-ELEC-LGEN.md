symptoms:
- SSGTG tripped off the line
- generator breaker opened
- one generator carrying the whole load
- load shedding occurred
- frequency and voltage dip
- lights flicker as a generator drops off
causes:
- SSGTG automatic shutdown
- generator breaker trip
- loss of fuel or lube oil to the SSGTG
- electrical fault on the generator
effects:
- overload of the remaining SSGTG
- load shedding of non-vital loads
- possible loss of all ship's service power

1. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-01] Verify at the EPCC which SSGTG tripped, that its generator breaker is open, and the load on the remaining SSGTG. requires: none
2. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-02] Report the casualty to the EOOW, the bridge, and the Chief Engineer. requires: 01
3. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-03] Verify that the standby SSGTG started automatically, synchronized, and closed on the bus. requires: 02
4. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-04] If the standby SSGTG did not start, start it from the EPCC or the SWBD and parallel it to the bus. requires: 03
5. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-05] Check the load on each on-line SSGTG, and check whether the turbine overload protection system shed load. requires: 03
6. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-06] Shed non-vital loads by hand if the SSGTG that took over the load is still overloaded. requires: 05. danger: do not let the remaining SSGTG run overloaded; an overloaded generator trips and the ship loses all power.
7. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-07] Restore the shed loads after two SSGTGs are on line and share the load. requires: 06
8. [step: EOCC-ELEC-LGEN-08] Find the cause on the tripped SSGTG with the casualty procedure for that alarm before it returns to service. requires: 07
